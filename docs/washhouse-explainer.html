washhouse

A cousin of tanktemp. It reads the hot-water meter, the cylinder probes, the boiler programmer and the boiler-room air temperature from MQTT. It turns metered litres into draws (how much, how fast, what kind, which tank), works out how much hot water is left, says whether each tank is heating, drawing or cooling, and flags water that never stops (creep). statehouse uses the draws as evidence that someone is home.

Everything on this page runs from a simulated weekday shaped by two weeks of tanktemp recordings and, since 20 Nov, the live hot-water meter. That data gives the real probe heights, loss rates, reheat powers and draw signatures, plus metered flows (taps 3.5–5, showers 4–6.4, baths 12–31 L/min hot), the right tank's three-quarter share of each draw, and a dribbling tap's creep. It is not a replay of the recordings, so it carries no house data. The estimator is a port of the tanktemp session's avail.py and matches it exactly (see the model).

1 · Where it sits

A derived layer on top of tanktemp, not a replacement

tanktemp keeps publishing raw probe readings, and statehouse and greenhouse keep consuming them unchanged. washhouse subscribes to the same feeds and publishes derived state under its own prefix. The moving dots show messages; hover a box for details.

One hot-water tree, one owner per subtree. Everything about hot water lives under water/hot/. washhouse owns the four named topics at the top, and tanktemp owns water/hot/tanktemp/…, its raw facts, which moved there from energy/hot_water in Nov 2026. Each publisher has its own status and LWT. statehouse's tanktemp adapter subscribes to water/hot/tanktemp/+/state and …/status, assuming one publisher per prefix. So washhouse never publishes inside tanktemp's subtree: a system/state there would be parsed as a cylinder called "system", and washhouse's LWT would mark all ten probes offline.

4 · Draws

Every draw in the day: metered, then explained by the probes

The meter decides. A draw starts at its first metered litre and ends after a minute with no litre. So litres less than a minute apart belong to one draw: a real bath paused 40 s mid-fill and stayed one draw. Its volume is the litre count, good to about ±1 L because of the partial litres at each end. Its flow is the median gap between litres. Its kind comes from flow and size: a bath runs at 10 L/min or more and is 20 L or more; a shower is 20 L or more over 3 min or longer at 3 L/min or more; anything else is a tap, including a slow steady run of 1–2 L/min. The probes then explain it: which tank supplied it, from each tank's heat lost over what a litre from its own outlet carries; the true outlet temperature, from P5's peak; and how much heat is left. The old probe-only heat balance is kept only as a cross-check. On the live data it came within 8 % of the meter for a 50 L shower from a layered tank, was 3–5× too high for small draws, and saw only 4 of 15 L when the cold layer sat between P2 (400 mm) and P3 (825 mm). It is marked unreliable during a reheat and below 20 L.

Which tank measured

Both outlets merge before the meter, but the tanks don't share equally: the right tank supplies about three quarters of every draw (2.26 of 2.95 kWh in a 50 L shower). Its P1 reacts within seconds and its P2 falls 20 °C or more, while the left's barely moves. Combined with its faster standing loss, the right tank runs low first, so "low hot water" is judged for the system, never one tank.

Small and masked draws measured

A litre at the basin is counted like any other. P1 dips even for 1–2 L, which makes the probe heat balance overshoot, and during a reheat the coil warms the inlet so P1 barely moves. Neither matters now: the meter counts both. A 72 L bath run during a reheat was metered exactly. Two nights of metering showed no 02:15 draw.

Creep, not a draw measured

In a real draw each litre holds the reed closed for 1–4 s. A tap left dribbling moves the pointer so slowly that a lone litre holds it closed for minutes (2–23 min, about 0.07 L/min, on 21 Nov). washhouse never counts those as draws or as occupancy. Two of them within 45 min raise continuous_flow: a tap left on, a leak, or water circulating through the meter.

5 · The estimate

From five temperatures to "litres of 40 °C water"

A port of avail.py. The vessel is a vertical cylinder with shallow spherical domes, solved to hold exactly 200 L. The temperature profile is linear between probe heights and flat beyond the end probes. The stratification correction takes a running maximum from the bottom up, because a still tank can't be colder above than below. That lifts the heat-sunk P5 and the right tank's return-water P4. Water at or above Tmin counts as usable, converted to litres at 40 °C when mixed with cold. Plug flow is assumed, so expect about 85–90 % in practice.

6 · On the wire

What washhouse publishes

This is a draft; docs/WIRE.md will be the normative version, with golden fixtures. On the input side, washhouse decodes tanktemp's meter as its WIRE.md defines it. It de-duplicates on (counting_since, seq), sums litres across counting runs (every tanktemp restart starts a new one), reads a jump in count as skipped messages, not lost litres, and knows the last sweep at shutdown matches no tank states. It keeps the boiler's HW/CH from heartbeats and events, because after a reconnect the retained …/system message can be a RECONNECTED event, not a heartbeat. On the output side it follows tanktemp's conventions: UTC RFC 3339 timestamp taken from the source sweep, every key always present, null for unknown (never 0), fixed key order, a schema id, and clock_synced. The payloads below follow the simulator's clock.

7 · statehouse

  
How the sister service uses it

  
These recommendations come from the statehouse session and the docs/hot-water-service.md design note on its claude/zen-hawking-ud04lv branch. They are changes to statehouse, each its own PR.

  

    
① A washhouse adapter
    
      
  • Built like the intercom adapter, on the engine clock.
    • 
      
  • draw_started → IngestSignal("hot_water_draw") with Since = started_at (not when it was detected), a 30 min safety TTL refreshed by each draw_updated, and an empty Location, because a draw can't be pinned to a room.
    • 
      
  • No confidence gate any more. A draw only exists once a litre has been metered, so every draw is real water. (House state ignores signal confidence anyway: any active signal means "occupied, 0.9".) Creep is never a draw: continuous_flow becomes a warning on the hot-water device, not occupancy.
    • 
      
  • draw_ended → ClearSignal plus UpdateActivity with the litres and kind.

    
② Tanks as devices
    
      
  • The cylinders stay telemetry:false placement entries.
    • 
      
  • washhouse becomes a device per tank: scheme washhouse, part_of the cylinder, the same pattern as the probes.
    • 
      
  • New model.Reading fields: usable_litres_40c and stored_kwh.
    • 
      
  • The new class (e.g. hot_water_system) is not occupancy-relevant; the draw signals carry occupancy.

    
③ Stop counting timers as people
    
      
  • boiler:hw and boiler:ch are binary_state_device, which counts toward occupancy and activity. So a morning HW timer says "someone's home". Seen live: the morning timers alone switch the house to "day" and "active" before anyone is up.
    • 
      
  • Move both to a scheduled-relay class that is not occupancy-relevant, and exclude them from activeCount.
    • 
      
  • Contact and motion sensors keep binary_state_device.

  

  
Order of rollout: statehouse_behaviour is decoded strictly (DisallowUnknownFields). Adding adapters.washhouse before statehouse knows the key would make it refuse the whole namespace. So: deploy statehouse, then add the key, then restart (SIGHUP doesn't start adapters). Note also that per-reading quality (#82) was deferred, so washhouse's quality field will be dropped by statehouse for now.







  
8 · Plan

  
How it gets built

  

    

      
0
Bootstrap. Repo, CLAUDE.md, and the tanktemp session's briefing committed as docs/HOT-WATER-SYSTEM.md with its confidence labels. CLAUDE.md carries tanktemp's working rules: gofmt; tests that fail first, with mutants that must fail them; concurrency tests run -race -count=200; an injected clock; no PII; every outside system behind an interface with a shared contract test; everything declared in config; golden files that pin key order; OpenAPI and README in the same change; scripts that enforce rollout order.

      
1
Fixtures, the meter decoder, the pure core. A tested script slices fixtures out of the live recordings. It fails on any LAN address or SSID, and it shifts every timestamp to a fake date, because draw times are an occupancy pattern. The fixtures carry metered litres: a shower, a short shower straight after it, two baths (one during a reheat), tap draws, a dribbling tap's creep, a quiet night, 06:30 reheats. Then the meter consumer in common/wire, against tanktemp's goldens; then vessel, profile and estimate, with parity tests against avail.py.

      
2
Meter draws, attribution, thermal. internal/meter: stitch counting runs, segment litres into draws, flow, kind, creep. internal/draw: which tank, outlet temperature, energy used, the heat balance as a flagged cross-check. Per-tank UA·(T−Troom) against the boiler-room sensor (about 230 W from the right tank, matching 15–19 W/K). A washreplay tool turns recordings into events, with golden tests on the metered fixtures.

      
3
Wire contract. docs/WIRE.md and goldens, reviewed with the statehouse and tanktemp sessions.

      
4
Shared code into sweeney/common. See the right-hand card.

      
5
Service shell. MQTT in and out, common/lifecycle, common/health /healthz, a JWT API (/api/v1/state, /tanks/{id}, /draws, /model), Influx, and a remote washhouse config namespace.

      
6
Deploy. On the home server. Idempotent scripts with --check and --yes, tested first. Commit dates settled before the build is stamped.

      
7
statehouse PRs ①②③ above, then later: forecasts (time to empty or full), daily usage, a programme advisor, and measuring the pipe-lagging before and after through the UA fit.

9 · Open questions

  
Things to settle

  

    
For the owner
    
      
  • Is the right tank's valve open on CH calls because of a manual lever, wiring, or a latch? It gives about 6 kWh/day of free top-up, but only in winter.
    • 
      
  • Is the gravity secondary loop real? Feel the return pipe late in the evening, or tape a spare DS18B20 to it.
    • 
      
  • Is the right tank really an STI 210, and what are its inner diameter and insulation thickness? These are the vessel model's biggest guesses.
    • 
      
  • Flow rates per outlet: now metered, at 3.5–5 L/min for taps, 4–6.4 for showers and 12–31 for baths (hot). Which tap is which still needs the owner.
    • 
      
  • The briefing's nightly draw: two metered nights showed none.
    • 
      
  • What makes the creep (lone litres every ~14 min, seen once so far)? A hot tap not fully closed, or circulation through the meter?
    • 
      
  • Why does the right tank take about three quarters of every draw: the pipe runs, or the inlet arrangement?
    •
